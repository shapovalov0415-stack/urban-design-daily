## Germany’s embattled nightlife scene welcomes plan to reclassify clubs

**Source:** Guardian Cities  
**URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/may/31/germany-nightlife-scene-reclassify-clubs-berlin  
**Topics:** Housing

The Guardian reports that Germany's federal government, under Chancellor Friedrich Merz, has approved a fundamental change to the country's building regulations that formally reclassifies nightclubs as venues with cultural and artistic value — distinct from the amusement and adult-entertainment categories they have shared in zoning code for decades. The change is consequential because zoning classification determines what owners and developers can do with the surrounding parcels: nightclubs previously sat in a planning regime that treated them as quasi-industrial nuisances, making it routine for landlords and developers to evict operators to clear sites for residential or commercial redevelopment. Berlin's Club Commission, RAW-Gelände collective and other industry advocates have lobbied for the change since the early 2010s, citing the closure of more than 100 venues across Berlin, Hamburg and Cologne over the last decade under combined pressure from rising rents, post-pandemic attendance shifts and noise complaints from new residential neighbours. The reclassification doesn't directly subsidise venues but materially raises the bar for displacing them, embedding what the Club Commission calls 'cultural infrastructure' protection into the planning framework. Implementation will fall to Länder governments translating the federal change into state building codes through the second half of 2026.

**Why it matters:** Nightlife and music-venue preservation is increasingly being argued as a planning question, not a cultural-policy one — and Germany's reclassification is the cleanest legal mechanism on the table for any city (including Melbourne, Sydney and Auckland) that wants to protect grassroots venues from straightforward zoning-driven displacement.

## Help to Buy goes live in Tasmania — final state to join federal shared-equity scheme

**Source:** Housing Australia  
**URL:** https://www.housingaustralia.gov.au/media/australian-government-help-buy-scheme-now-available-tasmania  
**Topics:** Australia, Tasmania, Hobart, Housing, Federal Policy

Housing Australia activated the Commonwealth's Help to Buy shared-equity scheme in Tasmania on 9 June 2026, making it the final state or territory to plug into the program and completing national coverage of the federal first-home pathway. Eligible Tasmanian buyers can secure a property with as little as a 2 percent deposit without lender's mortgage insurance, while the Commonwealth takes a federal equity stake of up to 40 percent for new builds and up to 30 percent for existing dwellings. The scheme caps annual taxable income at $100,000 for singles and $160,000 for joint applicants or single parents, with property-price ceilings of $700,000 in Hobart and $550,000 elsewhere in the state. Help to Buy was tested as a Commonwealth response to deposit-gap exclusion of working-age renters from ownership: by absorbing equity directly rather than subsidising stamp duty or grants, the government argues it can intervene at higher loan-to-value ratios without pushing first-home buyers into mortgage stress. The Tasmania rollout collides with the 30 June 2026 expiry of several existing state-level first-home incentives, meaning the federal scheme effectively backstops what the Tasmanian state government is winding back. National uptake to date has been concentrated in regional Victoria and outer-suburban Queensland, where the price caps line up with median dwelling values.

**Why it matters:** Shared-equity programs are now the dominant federal lever in Australian housing policy and Tasmania's onboarding completes the national footprint. The scheme's design — federal equity rather than grants — is the template state housing strategies will increasingly have to coordinate with, particularly as price caps interact with up-zoned mid-rise inventory in middle-ring suburbs.

## Idaho's big housing breakthrough year: ADUs, duplexes, starter homes legalised statewide

**Source:** Sightline Institute  
**URL:** https://www.sightline.org/2026/04/29/idahos-big-housing-breakthrough-year/  
**Topics:** United States, Idaho, Zoning Reform, ADU, Housing

Sightline Institute frames Idaho's 2026 legislative session as the state's most significant housing-supply reform in a generation, pointing to a coordinated package of bills covering ADUs, duplexes, starter homes, manufactured housing, single-stair apartment construction, permitting speed and lot-splitting. The headline measure, HB 800, requires every parcel zoned for multifamily housing to permit a manufactured duplex as small as 800 square feet — a floor effectively breaking the de-facto single-detached zoning that had survived decades of incremental reform. Companion bills shorten permitting timelines, override municipal owner-occupancy clauses on ADUs, and unlock a broader set of single-stair apartment typologies modelled on Cascadia design code precedents. Sightline argues Idaho's politics — a Republican legislature responding to severe price growth in Boise, Idaho Falls and Coeur d'Alene — show that pro-supply reform now scales beyond the West Coast YIMBY base and can find traction in fiscally conservative legislatures when framed as deregulation and property-rights restoration. The piece concludes by noting outcomes will depend on local council interpretation and whether smaller cities adopt model ordinances quickly, but the statutory floor is now permanent.

**Why it matters:** Idaho is the clearest current proof that 'gentle density' housing reform travels across the US partisan spectrum — useful evidence Australian planning advocates can point to when state-level upzoning is framed as a left-coded policy. The 800-square-foot manufactured-duplex floor is also a concrete template that smaller jurisdictions can copy without rewriting their planning scheme from scratch.
