## America's 'roundabout city' is in… Indiana?

**Source:** Planetizen  
**URL:** https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/06/137759-americas-roundabout-city-indiana  
**Topics:** United States, Indiana, Road Safety, Street Design, Traffic

Planetizen profiles Carmel, Indiana — a 110,000-person suburb of Indianapolis that over the past two decades has systematically replaced signalised intersections with more than 140 modern roundabouts, the highest density of any US city. Long-time mayor Jim Brainard, who left office in 2024 after seven terms, framed the conversions as a multi-objective intervention: federal data shows roundabouts reduce injury crashes by roughly 80 percent and fatal crashes by close to 90 percent compared with signalised intersections, while also cutting idling fuel use and carbon emissions because traffic flows continuously instead of stopping. The piece walks through how Carmel funded the program through Tax Increment Financing district revenue and engineering reviews that quantified avoided crash-injury and time-saving costs against capital outlay, building a politically durable pro-roundabout coalition of insurers, parents, fire chiefs and downtown retailers who gained pedestrian-scaled streets. The model has now been studied by departments of transportation across the US and replicated in pockets from Bend, Oregon to Loudoun County, Virginia. Critics point out that mid-block accessibility for pedestrians and cyclists requires design care — uncontrolled crossings can become harder for vulnerable users in high-speed multi-lane roundabouts — but Carmel's compact single-lane designs with raised crossings appear to have largely avoided that failure mode. The story lands amid a renewed national interest in low-cost street safety treatments as Vision Zero programs in larger cities struggle to scale.

**Why it matters:** Carmel turned an obscure intersection retrofit into a city brand, and the safety numbers (~80% fewer injury crashes) are the kind of evidence-based, low-capital intervention that Australian councils chasing Vision Zero outcomes can pitch to risk-averse engineers and budget committees without waiting for major capital programs.

## AI can design cities, but can it understand what matters to people? 10 ways to keep humans in control

**Source:** The Conversation AU  
**URL:** https://theconversation.com/ai-can-design-cities-but-can-it-understand-what-matters-to-people-10-ways-to-keep-humans-in-control-281471  
**Topics:** Urban Design

Researchers Abeer Elshater (Ain Shams University) and Hisham Abusaada (Housing and Building National Research Center), writing in The Conversation, propose 10 principles for keeping human judgment at the centre of AI-assisted urban design as planners increasingly fold large-language models into research and design workflows. The authors argue that the direction of inquiry must originate with the researcher — outsourcing problem-framing to AI produces generic outputs disconnected from place. They warn against treating AI outputs as verified truth (LLMs confidently hallucinate citations), against assuming context-portability (urban solutions are place-specific and require local social and political grounding), and against accepting default solutions uncritically (models often suggest outdated patterns like road-widening regardless of context). Researchers retain full responsibility for source validation; AI cannot replace critical judgment. The piece flags continuity limits — LLMs lose coherence across long projects — and recommends positioning AI as a thinking partner rather than a co-equal author, with planners deciding priorities, ethical frameworks and inclusion criteria. The final principle calls for strategic integration: combine AI insights with field data, community engagement and professional judgment rather than replacing design processes wholesale. The argument lands as Australian and international planning consultancies pilot LLM tools for site analysis, policy drafting and community-feedback synthesis, often without explicit governance for who validates the output.

**Why it matters:** Australian planning consultancies and councils are already piloting LLM tools without explicit governance over output validation; the 10 principles give a defensible checklist for project briefs, RFPs and ethics frameworks before AI quietly becomes the default first draft of public-realm decisions.

## Cities are making it rain more – but not as much as scientists thought

**Source:** The Conversation AU  
**URL:** https://theconversation.com/cities-are-making-it-rain-more-but-not-as-much-as-scientists-thought-284067  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, Melbourne, Climate Adaptation, Stormwater

Climate scientists Shankar Sharma, Andy Pitman and Jason Evans at UNSW Sydney have published research in The Conversation revising the long-running claim that cities measurably increase rainfall. Analysing NASA's IMERG satellite rainfall record across 15 major cities — Sydney and Melbourne included, alongside Lagos, London, Beijing, Berlin, Mexico City and Paris — the team finds rain events do occur more frequently over urban areas than the surrounding countryside, but the per-event intensity is typically lower, so the urban signal is one of frequency rather than volume. Crucially, they show that earlier studies were partly an artefact of satellite measurement change: microwave sensors detect raindrops directly while infrared sensors do not, and microwave observations were made nearly twice as often by 2023 as in 2001. When adjusted for sampling-frequency shifts, around 20 percent of the apparent long-term increase across the 15 cities disappears, and for Lagos, London, Melbourne, Beijing, Berlin, Mexico City and Paris over 40 percent of the apparent increase was attributable to better detection rather than real climate change. A genuine urban-rainfall effect remains after adjustment — driven by urban heat uplifting air, rougher built surfaces redirecting winds upward, and aerosols altering cloud droplet size — but it is smaller than previously reported and varies city by city.

**Why it matters:** Stormwater designers in Sydney and Melbourne plan to rainfall projections that bake in 'urban rainfall amplification'; this UNSW result tightens that assumption, which matters for drainage sizing, green-stormwater investment, and how aggressively councils argue for climate-justified infrastructure upgrades.
