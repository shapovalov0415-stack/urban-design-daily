## Victoria's planning minister approves Central Melbourne Design Guide

**Source:** ArchitectureAU  
**URL:** https://architectureau.com/articles/city-of-melbourne-urban-design-standards/  
**Topics:** Australia, Melbourne, Urban Design, Policy, Public Realm

Victoria's planning minister has approved the City of Melbourne's Central Melbourne Design Guide, which represents the biggest overhaul of the city's planning priorities since the 1990s. The guide focuses on the street level, with new requirements for active street frontages and design detail requirements for the lower levels of buildings to ensure high quality and human scale design. It also limits car parking at the lower 20 metres of buildings and limits the area at ground level occupied by building services. Deputy lord mayor Nicholas Reece stated the city is "raising the standard for development to protect our streets from featureless facades and our skyline from ugly towers that are nothing more than spreadsheets in the sky." The amendment proposes a new approach to urban design policy, including a new overlay which consolidates the existing planning scheme provisions and brings them in line with best practice. The amendment includes a new Central Melbourne Design Guide which helps the community, developers and planners to understand the policy requirements within the Melbourne Planning Scheme. The objective is to improve the quality of urban design outcomes in private development in the central city and Southbank. Key measures include extension of the successful active frontage requirements to new areas of the city, including Southbank, and new requirements to focus more specifically on the design detail of the lower levels of a building, to ensure high quality, human scale environments.

**Why it matters:** This signals a substantive shift toward quality-led urban design controls in Australia's second-largest city, prioritizing street-level activation and human-scale design over purely economic development outcomes—providing a potential model for other Australian capitals grappling with high-rise development pressures.

## Johns Hopkins updates their sprawl database

**Source:** Planetizen  
**URL:** https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/05/137562-johns-hopkins-updates-their-sprawl-database  
**Topics:** United States, Urban Form, Health, Sprawl, Research

In a follow up to their 2014 study, Johns Hopkins has released new data on urban sprawl in the U.S. and new findings on its health and wellbeing impacts. Supported by the Center for Smart Transportation and the Bloomberg American Health Initiative, the index defines, measures, and evaluates urban sprawl in the U.S. by measuring development density, land use mix, population and employment centering, and street connectivity. The study finds that sprawling residential living is linked to worse health outcomes, including higher rates of hospitalizations due to COPD and heart attacks; high rates of residential energy burden, increased rates of disconnected youth and social connections and even higher risks of Lyme disease infection. Unsurprisingly, the analysis found that less sprawling areas provide health benefits and offset living costs compared to areas that are more sprawling. The updated database provides metropolitan sprawl rankings, interactive maps and data, establishing an evidence base for linking urban form directly to public health and cost-of-living pressures. Released in May 2026, the refresh comes more than a decade after the baseline study and offers planners quantifiable metrics for evaluating the long-term consequences of low-density, auto-dependent development patterns.

**Why it matters:** This evidence-based tool quantifies the public health and economic costs of sprawl, providing urban designers with defensible metrics to advocate for compact, mixed-use development and challenge the social and fiscal externalities of low-density zoning.

## Maryland unlocks thousands of potential homes near transit

**Source:** Greater Greater Washington  
**URL:** https://ggwash.org/view/103392/maryland-legislature-passes-transit-oriented-development-preemption  
**Topics:** Maryland, Transit-Oriented Development, Zoning Reform, Policy, Housing

At the close of the 2026 legislative session, the Maryland legislature passed the Transit and Housing Opportunity Act (HB 894), one of several successful elements of Governor Wes Moore's housing package. The Act has several main components which will impact the future of transit-oriented development across the state, with the state government wresting control over some aspects of land use from local governments near certain rail transit stations. All WMATA heavy rail lines in Maryland, the Purple Line, the Baltimore/Maryland Transit Administration Light Rail and Metro, and the MARC Penn line (between New Carrollton and Baltimore Penn Station) are impacted—these lines are identified via a provision that a rail transit station must receive at least hourly service on average between 8:00 am and 6:00 pm on weekdays. In the most striking piece of the legislation, the state has preempted some local control of land use within one half mile of a rail station, with zoning regulations within the half mile buffer now required to allow mixed-use development on land designated for residential use or appropriate commercial use, defined as any combination of residential with recreational, office, dining, or retail use. The act exempts areas currently zoned as single-family residential, but will still impact numerous parcels currently zoned for only multifamily residential use near transit stations throughout the state. The Maryland Department of Transportation estimates that its land in the Baltimore region and along the MARC Penn Line could generate 7,000 housing units and $1.4 billion in state and local tax revenue.

**Why it matters:** Maryland's state-level preemption of local zoning near frequent transit represents one of the most assertive North American examples of linking land-use authority to infrastructure investment, creating a model for other states seeking to unlock TOD potential despite municipal resistance.
