## Housing Delivery Authority 12 month review finalised as pipeline grows to 117,000 homes

**Source:** NSW Planning Portal  
**URL:** https://www2.planning.nsw.gov.au/  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, NSW, Housing, Policy

The NSW Housing Delivery Authority has completed its 12-month review, revealing a growing pipeline of 117,000 homes across metropolitan Sydney. Eleven additional projects have been declared State Significant Development following HDA recommendations, fast-tracking approvals under NSW's revamped planning system. The announcement coincides with ongoing implementation of the Planning System Reforms Act 2025, which modernized the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 for the first time in a generation. The reforms embed climate change resilience into planning objects for the first time and legislate the HDA's role in boosting housing supply. The state government is rolling out reforms in stages over 12 months, including Low and Mid-Rise Housing Policy, the NSW Housing Pattern Book with pre-approved sustainable designs for terraces, townhouses and mid-rise apartments, and the Transport Oriented Development Program. The HDA works to identify and accelerate housing projects, streamlining pathways through complying development and state-led rezonings to meet the National Housing Accord target of 377,000 new homes in NSW over five years from mid-2024.

**Why it matters:** NSW's institutional reforms—creating a dedicated housing delivery authority with legislative teeth and state override powers—represent Australia's most aggressive planning intervention to date, demonstrating how coordination authorities can cut through fragmented local approvals when facing systemic supply shortfalls.

## FTA announces $28.5 million for Fiscal Year 2026 Pilot Program for Transit-Oriented Development Planning

**Source:** Federal Transit Administration  
**URL:** https://www.transit.dot.gov/TODPilot  
**Topics:** United States, TOD, Transit, Federal Policy, Planning Grants

The Federal Transit Administration announced approximately $28.5 million in competitive grants for its FY 2026 Pilot Program for Transit-Oriented Development Planning on May 11, 2026, with applications due July 10. The program provides funding to local communities to integrate land use and transportation planning around new fixed guideway or core capacity improvement transit projects seeking Capital Investment Grant funding. Eligible planning work must examine ways to improve economic development and ridership, foster multimodal connectivity and accessibility, improve transit access for pedestrian and bicycle traffic, engage the private sector, identify infrastructure needs, and enable mixed-use development near transit stations. The program supports the Department of Transportation's goals to build "Great American Communities" that maintain or expand capacity for all travel modes. Selection criteria include geographic diversity, project readiness, likelihood of the associated transit project being delivered, and alignment with DOT Order 2100.7 on sound economic analysis. There is no minimum or maximum grant award, and projects may receive less than requested amounts.

**Why it matters:** Federal TOD planning grants signal renewed federal commitment to integrating land use and transit investment at project inception rather than retrofitting development later—critical for maximizing ridership returns and avoiding the car-oriented catchments that plague many recent light rail lines.
