## ‘A neoliberal nightmare’: my ride on the Vegas Loop – Elon Musk’s answer to traffic jams

**Source:** Guardian Cities  
**URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/17/neoliberal-nightmare-my-ride-on-vegas-loop-elon-musk-answer-to-traffic-jams  
**Topics:** Urban Design

Ten years ago, after complaining that traffic was ‘driving him nuts’, Musk’s Boring Company began building underground tunnels to ease congestion on the roads. Did he overpromise and underdeliver? It’s another blindingly bright day in Las Vegas but I’m 30ft underground and strapped in for a rocket ride to the future. Actually, it’s a Tesla ride to the future, and not a self-driving one. And it’s pretty slow – my driver tells me the speed limit down here is 30mph. It’s also pretty short: the journey is over in a matter of minutes. In fact, the Vegas Loop is a pretty underwhelming experience: a brief trundle down a white-walled tunnel only slightly larger than the vehicle itself, lined by strips of LEDs that change colour every few seconds, in an attempt to inject some Vegas glitz. I’d been hoping to ask other Loop-riders what they made of the experience, but … there aren’t any. I’m the only person here. This is not the futuristic transport solution Elon Musk originally promised. ## Nine new mid-rise designs added to NSW Pattern Book to accelerate apartment supply

**Source:** ArchitectureAU  
**URL:** https://architectureau.com/articles/nine-mid-rise-designs-added-to-nsw-pattern-book/  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, NSW, Housing, Mid-Rise

The Minns Labor Government has released nine new mid-rise apartment designs as the next phase of the NSW Housing Pattern Book, building on the eight low-rise patterns published in July 2025. The new package — designed by leading practices from Australia and New Zealand — covers buildings of three to six storeys and is structured to suit different parcel geometries: four designs for small lots, three for large lots and two for corner lots, so a developer or council planner can lift a tested layout off the shelf rather than commission a bespoke scheme each time. Each pattern includes endorsed floor plates, façade options, sustainability features and a streamlined planning pathway that links the design directly into the new TOD SEPP and complying development pipelines, cutting months of assessment time for proposals that meet the design parameters. The government is subsidising uptake: Small Lot and Corner Lot Apartment patterns are priced at $15,000 per lot but discounted to $1,500 until 31 May 2026, and Large Lot Apartments at $25,000 per lot are discounted to $2,500 over the same window. The package follows the Housing Pattern Book's framing as the centrepiece of NSW's response to the National Housing Accord 1.2 million-homes target and Sydney's chronic shortfall in mid-density housing near rail and metro stations.

**Why it matters:** The mid-rise patterns turn 'gentle density near transit' from a slogan into a by-right delivery option — pre-endorsed designs, fast-tracked approvals, deeply subsidised licensing — and give other Australian jurisdictions a concrete template to copy if they want to lift apartment supply without re-litigating every façade.

## Which US cities are succeeding at building housing near transit

**Source:** Bloomberg CityLab  
**URL:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/these-us-cities-are-succeeding-in-building-housing-near-transit  
**Topics:** United States, Transit-Oriented Development, Housing, Affordable Housing, Urban Form

Bloomberg CityLab reports on a new Urban Institute analysis that ranks US metro regions by how much new housing is actually being delivered within walking distance of frequent transit — and finds the gap between rhetoric and outcomes is enormous. New York and San Francisco have concentrated the most growth around rail and bus-rapid-transit stations, while Atlanta and Dallas have captured comparatively little of their transit investment as housing supply. The piece spotlights Jersey City's new 'Building for Working Families' policy, which ties city support to a 20 percent affordability requirement, union labour and community-benefits agreements, and Atlantic Square in Miami's Overtown — 616 apartments including 360 workforce and affordable units beside Brightline and Metrorail service, now the largest mixed-income transit-oriented development in Miami-Dade County. Smaller projects illustrate the pattern as well: Triton Pointe in St. Louis County (192 affordable units near a Metrolink light-rail station) and Lazul West in Jersey City (202 units beside Hudson-Bergen Light Rail). The reporting lands as the proposed BUILD America 250 Act threatens to cut federal transit funding by 15 percent and new-project funding by 45 percent, making the question of which cities can actually convert transit dollars into housing newly urgent.

**Why it matters:** Transit-oriented housing is now a measurable outcome, not a planning aspiration; the rankings give urban designers a benchmarking lens for whether their region's rail and BRT investments are translating into homes, and identify which policy levers (city subsidy + affordability + labour terms) the leaders are using.
