## SEFA commits $500m to social-impact finance; Victoria-Tasmania native logging shift sparks $205m public-cost review

**Source:** The Fifth Estate  
**URL:** https://thefifthestate.com.au/jobs-and-biz-news/sefa-in-new-500-million-fund-victoria-tasmania-forest-woes-eu-battery-swaps-deakin-uni/  
**Topics:** Australia, Affordable Housing, Social Finance, Public Realm, Climate Adaptation

The Fifth Estate's 25 June 2026 roundup leads with Social Enterprise Finance Australia (SEFA) announcing a $500 million social-impact funding commitment to 2030, structured to direct 37 per cent toward homelessness and affordable-housing initiatives and the balance across women's safety and mental-health services. The fund targets approximately 1,000 underfunded organisations, with explicit equity allocations — 50 per cent to female-led groups, 18 per cent First Nations-led, and 39 per cent regional — and deploys 'impact stacking' that layers grants, private capital and philanthropy in a single transaction. Nightingale Housing is cited as the template: speculative profit removed, affordability, environmental performance and long-term liveability prioritised. The second item dissects Victoria's response to its native-forest logging ban, which has seen state-owned forestry operations redirect harvesting to Tasmania under $10 million annual subsidies; an ABC Four Corners investigation cited by ANU Professor David Lindenmayer estimates the state would be $205 million better off without native logging and calls for a Royal Commission. Deakin University launched an ARC Training Centre (circAlloy) with $4.9 million over five years for sustainable alloy lifecycle management — directly relevant to construction-sector decarbonisation. The European Swaptopus venture (CATL × Octopus Energy) plans 30-plus electric-truck battery-swap hubs by 2035, the kind of freight infrastructure that will reshape industrial-precinct planning over the next decade.

**Why it matters:** The SEFA item is the most consequential urban-design signal in the bulletin: a $500m social-finance vehicle explicitly engineered around housing and Nightingale-style not-for-speculation delivery is the kind of capital stack Australian councils and CHPs need to operationalise mid-rise affordable supply at scale.

## NYC unveils $90 million capital plan to make Jackson Heights' Paseo Park a permanent linear park

**Source:** Paseo Park / NYC DOT  
**URL:** https://www.paseopark.org/news/alliance-for-paseo-park-unveils-community-roadmap-amp-renderingsnbspto-transform-nycs-gold-standard-open-street  
**Topics:** United States, New York, Walkable City, Public Realm, Landscape Urbanism

New York City has committed $90 million in capital funding to convert 34th Avenue in Jackson Heights, Queens — branded Paseo Park since its COVID-era Open Street designation — into the city's first permanent linear park, with detailed design now released by the NYC Department of Transportation in collaboration with WXY architecture + urban design and the Alliance for Paseo Park. The 26-block, 1.3-mile corridor running between 69th Street and Junction Boulevard will widen the central median for play, rest and exercise; raise the street bed level with the adjacent sidewalk; and elevate every crosswalk to give pedestrians and cyclists continuous priority over the cross-street vehicle network. The design organises the corridor into three block typologies tuned to adjacent land use: school blocks fronting Jackson Heights' seven public schools (designed around play structures and skate elements); active blocks for large community gatherings; and passive blocks for quieter neighbourhood use. The corridor already serves as the safe-passage route for roughly 7,000 children daily and is credited by NYC DOT with a 42 per cent reduction in traffic crashes since the open-street rules took effect. Construction is staged across the next several years; the permanent design replaces a temporary regime that has run on bollards and signage since 2020, locking the geometry in against the political cycles that periodically threaten Open Streets elsewhere in the city.

**Why it matters:** Paseo Park is the first U.S. test of converting a pandemic-era Open Street into capital-funded permanent public realm. The $90m commitment plus block-typology design gives Australian council advocates a working precedent for how to argue from pilot to permanence — directly relevant to Sydney's George Street, Melbourne's laneway program, and Brisbane's CBD trials.

## Rewilding the City: 6 Unbuilt Projects from the ArchDaily Community

**Source:** ArchDaily Cities  
**URL:** https://www.archdaily.com/1042738/rewilding-the-city-6-unbuilt-projects-from-the-archdaily-community  
**Topics:** Global, Landscape Urbanism, Rewilding, Public Realm, Climate Adaptation

ArchDaily's 26 June 2026 selection showcases six unbuilt projects from its global community that frame ecological restoration as an urban-design strategy rather than a landscape add-on. The selection spans five continents: HAS design and research's UK Grand Crystal Palace proposes a 3-kilometre rewilded ring for South London; Studio Symbiosis's Urban Oasis regenerates Hyderabad's Durgam Cheruvu lake through hydrology-led restoration; Chuan Liu's The Forgotten weaves botanical revival into a Suffolk peri-urban site as a temporal narrative; Buffa Studio's Urban Canopy in Constanta, Romania, embeds modular pergolas with native planting into car-park and transit zones; JOÃO TELES atelier with Bernardo Alvarado's NIDO in Tulum, Mexico, organises sensory connection through organic form and local materials; and DKA's Bachelet Park Square in Maglie, Italy, retrofits a town square as a multispecies habitat. Across the six, common moves include integrated water systems, native-species reintroduction, and modular green infrastructure that can be added to existing streets without major engineering. ArchDaily editorially frames the selection around what one project calls 'ecological choreography' — design tied to ecosystem timescales rather than construction cycles, prioritising site-specific terrain, hydrology and existing biodiversity over the standardised solutions that dominate competition urbanism. The compilation positions the discipline as shifting toward multispecies thinking and incremental ecological recovery embedded inside existing urban fabric, rather than large-scale displacement-driven masterplans.

**Why it matters:** The collection reads as a manifesto for a soft-city / landscape-urbanism turn: every project treats ecological restoration as urban form, not green dressing. For Melbourne students currently navigating studio briefs on heat, drought and biodiversity, the six precedents are directly transferable references.
