## Transit-Oriented Development Street Designation Proposed to Foster Pedestrian-Friendly Corridors

**Source:** City of Chicago  
**URL:** https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dcd/provdrs/planning_and_policydivision/news/2026/may/transit-oriented-development-street-designation-proposed-to-fost.html  
**Topics:** United States, Chicago, TOD, Zoning Reform, Public Realm, Transit

Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson introduced a measure to City Council on May 20, 2026, that would create a new Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Streets zoning designation to promote walkable, mixed-use development and higher-density housing along commercial corridors near transit. Corridors located within half a mile of a CTA or Metra train station, or within a quarter-mile of qualifying CTA bus lines, would be eligible for the designation. The TOD Street framework establishes zoning standards that encourage pedestrian-oriented development while explicitly prohibiting automobile-oriented uses including drive-throughs, vehicle sales, gas stations, and similar businesses. The designation represents a broader and more flexible tool than Chicago's existing Pedestrian Street (P-Street) designation, which has restrictive eligibility criteria that exclude many business areas. By expanding the scope of transit-supportive zoning, Chicago aims to reverse decades of auto-centric commercial development patterns and channel new investment toward transit-accessible, walkable neighborhoods. The measure arrives as part of Chicago's ongoing effort to increase density and ridership around its extensive CTA and Metra networks.

**Why it matters:** Prohibiting auto-oriented uses near transit represents a fundamental shift in corridor planning, moving beyond density bonuses to actively reshape the ground-floor experience and commercial ecology of transit-adjacent streets.

## Living on the Edge: Finding Feasibility in a Fluid Market

**Source:** The Urban Developer  
**URL:** https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/feasibility-crunch-australian-apartments-projects-2026  
**Topics:** Australia, Housing, Feasibility, Policy, Development

Australia is experiencing a critical feasibility crisis, with only 26 per cent of apartments that secured planning approval since 2020 proceeding to construction by the end of 2025, according to Urbis partner David Cresp. The figure remains below 30 per cent across all monitored cities—Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, and the Gold Coast. This dramatic gap between approvals and delivery is exacerbating the nation's housing shortage as construction costs, financing constraints, and market volatility combine to render projects unviable. The article explores how developers across Australia are navigating the challenging economics of apartment delivery, examining the structural barriers preventing approved projects from breaking ground. With the nation working toward ambitious housing targets under the National Housing Accord, the feasibility crunch represents a fundamental bottleneck: planning reform alone cannot solve housing supply if economic conditions make construction untenable. The piece investigates the dynamics around build-to-rent, social and affordable housing mandates, non-bank lending, and the interplay between planning approvals, development finance, and construction economics in a market where feasibility margins have evaporated for all but the most carefully calibrated projects.

**Why it matters:** This data reveals a critical failure point in Australia's housing delivery system—planning approvals mean little if projects can't achieve financial feasibility. Urban designers and planners must reckon with the economic constraints that determine what actually gets built, not just what gets approved.
