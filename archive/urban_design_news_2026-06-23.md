## Australian Urban Design Awards 2026: ecological landscapes and gentle transformation lead

**Source:** Landscape Australia  
**URL:** https://landscapeaustralia.com/articles/winners-revealed-2026-australian-urban-design-awards--/  
**Topics:** Australia, Melbourne, Victoria, Landscape Urbanism, Public Realm

The 2026 Australian Urban Design Awards has named twelve winners from over 80 entrants spanning four categories — Built Outcomes, Research and Advocacy, Strategic Design and Policy, and Urban Design Champions — with the steering committee chair describing this year's slate as marked by a 'gentler approach to urban transformation' rooted in Indigenous and community leadership, ecological landscapes and the creation of places for people to gather. Among the named projects, Jackson Clements Burrows Architects with Site Office Landscape Architecture and AW Maritime won for the St Kilda Pier Redevelopment in Victoria, an exercise in layering maritime heritage, public realm and ecological restoration on a contested foreshore. Hill Thalis Architecture and Urban Projects was recognised for the Campbelltown Station Commuter Car Park in NSW, deliberately structured for adaptive transformation into apartments, offices, or a cultural centre as TOD demand intensifies around the station. The Urban Land Institute Australia and the Victorian Department of Transport and Planning shared a Strategic Design and Policy award for the Net Zero Imperative Technical Assistance Panel report 'Street Trees as Essential Infrastructure,' which reframes canopy as infrastructure rather than amenity. Together the winners signal the discipline's centre of gravity in Australia: away from object-buildings and toward landscape, public realm, and convertible building typologies.

**Why it matters:** The 2026 winners read as a manifesto for a soft-city turn in Australian practice — pier landscapes, convertible commuter parks, and a canopy-as-infrastructure policy paper. For Melbourne practitioners, the Street Trees report alone is a brief-grade reference that legitimises green infrastructure budgets at council and state level.

## From London to Houston: four pedestrianisation projects reshaping walkable cities

**Source:** ArchDaily  
**URL:** https://www.archdaily.com/1037458/from-london-to-houston-four-ongoing-pedestrianisation-initiatives-shaping-more-walkable-cities  
**Topics:** United States, United Kingdom, Walkable City, Pedestrianisation, Public Realm

ArchDaily profiles four pedestrianisation initiatives at different stages of delivery across London, Houston, New York and one continental European peer, threaded together by the same recognition that street space remains a city's largest and most undervalued public asset. Houston's downtown pedestrian project is scheduled to complete in June 2026 in time for the city's hosting of FIFA Men's World Cup matches — an unusually fast civic timeline driven by external deadline, retrofitting downtown blocks with raised crossings, plaza interventions and continuous shaded routes between transit and stadium districts. New York's first permanent Open Street is being established on Jackson Heights' 34th Avenue, formalising the COVID-era experiment with hardscape changes that prioritise pedestrians, shared streets, plazas, landscaping and simplified through-corridor design. Ongoing community workshops are refining the geometry into early 2026. The London entry centres on the City Corporation's continued vehicle reduction in the Square Mile, treating pedestrianisation as a phased land-use change rather than a one-off event. The pieces share a methodological lesson: cities that succeeded made the temporary intervention legible as design, not closure, and used the trial period to socialise the geometry before locking it in. The article reads as a comparative reference for jurisdictions still litigating pilot vs. permanent.

**Why it matters:** Australian capitals (Sydney's George Street, Melbourne's laneways, Brisbane's CBD trial) are mid-conversation on exactly the pilot-to-permanent question this piece anatomises. The Jackson Heights and Houston playbooks give Melbourne advocates an evidence-based answer to 'what does locked-in look like.'

## Placemaking Week 2026 returns to Detroit anchored on the 27.5-mile Joe Louis Greenway

**Source:** Project for Public Spaces  
**URL:** https://www.pps.org/article/get-ready-for-placemaking-week-2026-in-detroit  
**Topics:** United States, Detroit, Placemaking, Landscape Urbanism, Public Realm

Project for Public Spaces has announced the fifth International Placemaking Week will run in Detroit from 24-26 June 2026 under the theme 'From Recovery to Resilience through Placemaking,' centred on the Joe Louis Greenway as a live case study of city-scale landscape-led recovery. The greenway is a 27.5-mile multi-use trail weaving across more than 23 Detroit neighbourhoods, threading together parks, schools, commercial districts, transit hubs and historic gateways into a single continuous public-realm spine. PPS positions the project as a working example of how mid-sized post-industrial cities can use landscape infrastructure as the connective tissue between equity, mobility and ecological repair, rather than treating each as a separate program. The conference programme is built around walkable site visits to greenway segments at varied stages of completion, paired with workshops on Indigenous land acknowledgement in placemaking practice, climate resilience through public space, and the operational economics of long, linear assets. Speakers are drawn from PPS's global placemaking network, with field hosts from Detroit Riverfront Conservancy, the City of Detroit's Planning Department, and community-based organisations along the greenway corridor.

**Why it matters:** Long, linear landscape-led recovery projects are quietly becoming the default for post-industrial cities, and Detroit's Joe Louis Greenway is the cleanest mid-sized example to date. Australian regional centres weighing similar 'green spine' moves can study the Greenway's economics and equity framing as a directly transferable reference.
