## Waterloo South renewal moves to public exhibition with 3,300 homes, half social or affordable

**Source:** NSW Government  
**URL:** https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/waterloo-south-application-public-exhibition  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, NSW, Affordable Housing, Urban Renewal

The Minns Labor Government has placed the concept development and rezoning application for Waterloo South on public exhibition, signalling the next major stage in the renewal of one of inner Sydney's largest public housing estates. The proposal sets out a vision for a vibrant new inner-city neighbourhood of approximately 3,300 new homes, with at least half delivered as social or affordable housing — a ratio significantly higher than typical urban renewal precincts in NSW and a direct response to the state's chronic shortage of below-market dwellings within reach of the CBD. Since 2023, the government has fully funded the project, completed the first stage of resident relocations, and lodged the Concept State Significant Development Application alongside the updated rezoning proposal, putting Waterloo South on the same fast-track pathway as the broader State Significant Rezoning Policy that streamlines complex city-shaping precincts. The exhibition period closes on 2 June 2026, with the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure assessing community feedback before finalising the controls. For practitioners, the package is notable for pairing a deep social/affordable mandate with high-density urban form near the new Waterloo Metro Station, a critical test of whether public land can deliver mixed-income transit-oriented housing at scale without sacrificing tenure mix.

**Why it matters:** Waterloo South is the highest-profile test in Australia of whether a state government can rebuild a major public housing estate at higher density while keeping social and affordable homes at 50 percent — a benchmark that will shape every comparable renewal pipeline in NSW and beyond.

## NSW announces four state-led Sydney rezonings to enable around 11,000 new homes

**Source:** NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure  
**URL:** https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/news/four-rezonings-to-enable-thousands-of-new-homes-across-sydney  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, NSW, Transit-Oriented Development, Housing

The NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure has confirmed four state-led rezonings expected to unlock about 11,000 new homes across Sydney, packaging them with the 28 state-significant rezonings already declared under the Housing Delivery Authority for a combined pipeline approaching 56,000 homes and 71,000 jobs. The four sites are Glenfield West (an 85-hectare Landcom-led precinct of terraces, apartments and detached houses with 2.8 hectares of new public open space, anchored by Glenfield train station and the M5); Tallawong Town Centre (31.8 hectares around Tallawong Metro Station, planned for higher-density mixed use, shops, community facilities and a new public school opening 2026); Warwick Farm (a 3.9-hectare Homes NSW site on Mannix Parade delivering social housing and a local park, served by Warwick Farm station and adjacent to Liverpool Hospital, Western Sydney University and Liverpool TAFE); and Westmead South Precinct (125 hectares south of Westmead Metro Station offering affordable housing inside a health-and-innovation district near Westmead Hospital and The Children's Hospital). Deputy Secretary Monica Gibson framed the package as accelerating housing choice, open space and jobs across south and north-west Sydney, with assessments coordinated through the State Significant Rezoning Policy and the Social and Affordable Housing Program.

**Why it matters:** The four sites lock in a state-led, transit-anchored upzoning model — four parcels, four metro/rail stations, ~11,000 homes — and demonstrate how NSW intends to convert public land near new metro infrastructure into housing supply at speed.

## Study: Urban Institute analysis underestimates zoning reform impacts

**Source:** Planetizen  
**URL:** https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/05/137482-study-urban-institute-analysis-underestimates-zoning-reform-impacts  
**Topics:** United States, Zoning Reform, Housing, Research, Policy

Planetizen reports on new research challenging a widely cited Urban Institute dataset that purported to show zoning reform has had limited impact on housing supply. The critique re-examines the Urban Institute's catalogue of 180 zoning changes across US municipalities and finds that 178 of them do not qualify as 'major reforms' on closer inspection — they are either too minor (e.g. single-parcel adjustments, parking-only tweaks) to plausibly affect housing supply, or the policy detail is too thin to evaluate, leaving the headline conclusion resting on an extremely small effective sample. The reanalysis argues this matters because pundits and state legislators have used the Urban Institute figures to claim that even ambitious upzoning produces little new housing, undercutting the political case for further reform. By contrast, when the dataset is filtered to genuinely substantive reforms (broad multifamily allowances, citywide upzoning, transit-corridor density bonuses), the housing-supply effects line up closely with the YIMBY-leaning economic literature. The piece lands amid intensifying federal and state debate over how aggressively zoning should be liberalised, and recommends planners interrogate the policy granularity of any 'zoning reform' study before drawing supply conclusions.

**Why it matters:** Zoning-reform impact estimates are now political ammunition; this study reminds urban designers and policy advocates to read past headline figures and check what actually counts as a 'reform' before letting evidence drive — or block — the next round of upzoning decisions.
