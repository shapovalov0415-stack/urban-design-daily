## UIA 2030 Award Recognises Global Projects Advancing UN Sustainable Development Goals

**Source:** ArchDaily  
**URL:** https://www.archdaily.com/1041666/the-2026-edition-of-the-uia-2030-award-honors-built-projects-advancing-the-un-sustainable-development-goals  
**Topics:** International, Awards, Sustainable Development, Community Planning, Heritage, Affordable Housing

During a ceremony at the World Urban Forum in Baku, Azerbaijan, on May 20, 2026, the International Union of Architects (UIA) and UN-Habitat announced winners of the third cycle of the UIA 2030 Award, recognising built projects that meaningfully contribute to achieving the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. The biennial award, which launched its third cycle in July 2025 with regional finalists announced in January 2026, invited submissions across six categories: sustainable water management, safe working environments, adequate and affordable housing, efficient and inclusive planning, access to green and public space, and climate resilience. Notable winners include the Hutong Community Renewal by Architectural Design and Research Institute of Tsinghua University, honoured for its sensitive, community-led approach to incremental renewal within Beijing's historic hutong fabric, demonstrating how small-scale interventions can generate lasting urban transformation while balancing conservation with adaptation through co-governance and resident participation. The Kahawa West Health Centre in Kenya by Kounkuey Design Initiative was recognised for its participatory design process and role as a civic, economic, and social anchor extending beyond healthcare provision. The award ceremony occurred alongside the World Urban Forum, positioning architecture and urban design as critical instruments for addressing pressing environmental, social, and economic challenges at city scale through replicable, methodologically clear interventions.

**Why it matters:** The UIA 2030 Award elevates built exemplars of community-led, incremental urbanism that address SDG targets through participatory processes and adaptive reuse—demonstrating practical, replicable methodologies for climate-responsive, socially grounded urban design in diverse global contexts.

## City of Sydney opens consultation on housing affordability discussion paper

**Source:** ArchitectureAU  
**URL:** https://architectureau.com/articles/city-of-sydney-opens-consultation-on-housing-affordability-discussion-paper/  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, Housing, Policy, Urban Infill

The City of Sydney has released its Housing for All discussion paper, launching public consultation on strategies to address mounting housing affordability pressures in Australia's largest city. The paper identifies ten locations across the local government area where additional housing could be delivered, including Ultimo and Pyrmont, the North Green Square precinct, Prince Alfred Park east, and the Supa Centa site at Moore Park. Further opportunities are outlined along the Broadway corridor and at Parramatta Road in Forest Lodge, as well as in Beaconsfield. The proposal also explores broader approaches including increasing housing along high streets, near parks, and in laneways—tactics that represent a shift toward fine-grained intensification within established urban fabric. The discussion paper forms part of the city's wider effort to address the supply shortfall driving affordability pressures in Sydney. The consultation period invites community feedback on where and how additional housing should be delivered across the municipality, signalling council's intent to play a more proactive role in directing growth within its boundaries. The initiative comes as Sydney grapples with population growth projected to add 1.2 million people over the next two decades, placing intensifying pressure on existing housing stock and infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** This signals a municipal-scale effort to identify and activate infill sites within a constrained inner-city context, offering urban designers insight into how local governments are navigating site selection, community engagement, and incremental densification strategies in established neighbourhoods.

## Maryland Legislature Passes Transit-Oriented Development Preemption

**Source:** The Transit Friendly Planning Newsletter (NJ TOD)  
**URL:** https://www.njtod.org/news-may-15-2026/  
**Topics:** United States, Maryland, Transit-Oriented Development, Zoning, Policy, Housing

Maryland has enacted the Transit and Housing Opportunity Act, a sweeping piece of state legislation that overrides local zoning and parking requirements near rail stations to stimulate transit-oriented, mixed-use development across the state. The law exempts state-owned and WMATA (Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority)-owned land near transit stations from local zoning controls, including land use classifications and density restrictions. This preemption approach represents a significant shift in the balance of planning authority, removing local government discretion over key development parameters in transit-adjacent locations in favour of state-level directives aimed at increasing housing supply and transit ridership. The Act is part of Maryland's broader strategy to address housing affordability and leverage public transit infrastructure to support higher-density, walkable urban forms. By mandating reduced parking minimums and loosening density caps, the legislation seeks to unlock development potential on publicly owned sites that have historically been underutilised or constrained by conservative local planning frameworks. The move positions Maryland among a growing cohort of U.S. states deploying preemption as a tool to bypass local resistance to intensification near transit nodes.

**Why it matters:** State-level preemption of local zoning authority near transit stations is an emerging model for accelerating TOD at scale, offering lessons—and cautionary tales—for jurisdictions worldwide grappling with how to balance regional housing goals against local planning autonomy.
