## Maryland legislature passes Transit and Housing Opportunity Act to unlock 7,000 homes near rail stations

**Source:** Office of Governor Wes Moore  
**URL:** https://governor.maryland.gov/news/press/pages/Governor-Moore-Celebrates-Passage-of-Entire-2026-Legislative-Agenda,-Delivering-on-Commitment-to-Protect-Marylanders,-Lower.aspx  
**Topics:** USA, Maryland, TOD, Zoning, Housing

Maryland Governor Wes Moore has celebrated passage of the Maryland Transit and Housing Opportunity Act as part of his entire 2026 legislative agenda, following adjournment of the state's legislative session in early May. The legislation leverages at least 300 acres of station-adjacent land to enable production of more than 7,000 new housing units and is projected to unlock nearly $1.4 billion in state and local tax revenue. The Act eliminates minimum parking requirements for developments within a quarter-mile of high-frequency rail stations, prohibits local zoning limitations on state-owned land within half a mile of qualifying rail stations, and automatically designates certain transit-oriented developments as Enterprise Zones to access property and income tax credits. Critically, the bill delays collection of development impact fees and excise taxes until occupancy rather than during permitting or early construction, improving project cash flow. The Maryland Department of Transportation estimates that current restrictive zoning on transit-adjacent state-owned land in the Baltimore region alone is preventing 2,700 housing units from being built. The legislation also directs the Maryland Economic Development Corporation to prioritize TOD redevelopment in its Strategic Infrastructure Revolving Loan Program.

**Why it matters:** Maryland's preemption of local zoning near transit demonstrates a state-scale mechanism to overcome municipal resistance to density, coupling land use reform with financial incentives that address the timing of fee collection—a cash-flow barrier frequently cited by TOD developers.

## Maryland Passes Transit and Housing Opportunity Act

**Source:** Greater Greater Washington  
**URL:** https://ggwash.org/view/91827/maryland-legislature-passes-transit-oriented-development-preemption  
**Topics:** Maryland, Transit-Oriented Development, Zoning, Housing, Policy

Maryland has passed the Transit and Housing Opportunity Act, overriding some local zoning and parking requirements near rail stations to encourage more transit-oriented, mixed-use development. The legislation, reported by Nick Finio and Natalie Roach for Greater Greater Washington on May 14, 2026, represents one of the most ambitious state-level TOD preemptions in the United States. The law exempts some state- and WMATA-owned land near transit from local zoning controls, including land use classifications and density restrictions, giving state and regional transit agencies greater authority to shape development around their stations. Local parking requirements near rail stations are also overridden to reduce car dependency and facilitate denser housing near high-quality transit. The act builds on Governor Wes Moore's earlier executive order to activate housing construction on state-owned land near transit stations. Maryland officials had previously unveiled plans to support building close to 5,000 new homes near transit stations as part of the Baltimore Regional Transit-Oriented Development Strategy, focusing on 134 acres of state-owned land, mostly parking, around transit stations. Among the first areas targeted is the Rogers Avenue Metro Station in Baltimore, where the state is seeking a developer for about 9 acres of state-owned land around the station, with the potential for 400 new homes and $27 million in state and city tax revenue. The legislation positions Maryland at the forefront of states using preemption to unlock transit-adjacent housing supply amid widespread affordability pressures.

**Why it matters:** Maryland's state-level zoning preemption near transit stations demonstrates how upper-tier governments can overcome local resistance to densification, potentially offering a replicable model for jurisdictions where municipal planning reform has stalled despite urgent housing need and existing transit infrastructure investment.
