## Melbourne Wants Faster, Simpler Planning

**Source:** The Urban Developer  
**URL:** https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/city-of-melbourne-planning-scheme-review-recommendations  
**Topics:** Australia, Melbourne, Victoria, Planning Reform, Housing

The City of Melbourne has unanimously endorsed a compulsory four-yearly review of the Melbourne Planning Scheme under Section 12B of the Victorian Planning and Environment Act 1987, sending the package of recommendations to Planning Minister Sonya Kilkenny and the Department of Transport and Planning. Deputy Lord Mayor Nicholas Reece noted council had already implemented the bulk of the 2018 review — an adopted affordable housing policy, environmentally sustainable design standards, titled zones, municipal development contributions plans, and updated sign policies that protect Aboriginal heritage. The new recommendations push much further: a re-examination of Docklands precinct controls, a state-level affordable housing policy backed by mandatory delivery tools, an increase in the gross floor area trigger by 25,000 square metres so larger projects are assessed at state rather than council level, a dedicated industrial land supply policy, a new Aboriginal Cultural Recognition / Designing with Country framework, refined motorcycle parking definitions, increased state-wide bicycle parking rates, and secured community infrastructure for the rapidly developing Arden precinct. Council framed the review as a chance to modernise controls for a city under acute housing pressure while embedding cultural recognition and active-transport infrastructure into the statutory framework.

**Why it matters:** Melbourne is using its statutory planning review window to push housing affordability tools, Country-based design, and active-transport ratios up to state level — a model other Australian councils may follow as the Housing Accord deadline approaches.

## LA Delays State Housing Mandate With 'Low-Rise Ordinance'

**Source:** Planetizen  
**URL:** https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/06/137723-la-delays-state-housing-mandate-low-rise-ordinance  
**Topics:** United States, Los Angeles, Transit-Oriented Development, Zoning Reform, Housing

The Los Angeles City Council has approved a 'Low-Rise Ordinance' designed to slow the rollout of California's SB 79, the state law that takes effect in July 2026 and allows apartment buildings up to nine stories near rail and bus-rapid-transit stops. SB 79 lets cities defer full implementation until 2030 if they produce their own credible plans for boosting transit-area housing supply in the meantime, and LA's ordinance is the first major city's response. The new ordinance permits buildings up to four stories near transit stops across 57 neighbourhoods and explicitly carves out areas the council classifies as historically significant, at high wildfire risk, or 'low resource', triggering accusations from YIMBY advocates that the carve-outs collectively shield much of the city from real upzoning. Supporters at City Hall argue it threads the needle of state compliance with neighbourhood-scale design, while housing economists warn the four-story cap delivers a fraction of the units SB 79 was designed to unlock. SB 79 itself bundles affordability requirements, prevailing-wage labour standards, and fire-risk exceptions, and is widely seen as California's most consequential transit-housing law in a decade.

**Why it matters:** When a city the size of LA dilutes a flagship state transit-housing law within weeks of its commencement, it tests whether 'localism' becomes a loophole that other big-city councils will copy — and reshapes the implementation maths for Australian policymakers watching California's TOD experiment.

## Pyrmont Planning Overhaul to Reshape Sydney's Inner-West

**Source:** The Urban Developer  
**URL:** https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/ultimo-pyrmont-strategy-sydney-decision-council-metro-progress-rezoning  
**Topics:** Australia, Sydney, NSW, Transit-Oriented Development, Affordable Housing

Sydney City Council is poised to vote on the Ultimo-Pyrmont Peninsula Strategy, a 142-hectare planning framework between Broadway and Pirrama Road developed over three years in collaboration with the NSW Department of Planning. The strategy converts a state-level vision into enforceable local controls and targets 4,555 to 4,817 new homes alongside 23,637 to 27,004 jobs — both up from the 2024 draft of roughly 4,000 homes and 23,000 jobs — with a separate Stage 2 investigation potentially unlocking another 1,300 dwellings. About 15 hectares clustered around the future Pyrmont Metro station (opening 2032) and Harris Street will receive enhanced height and density permissions under new mixed-use zoning, and two priority sites at 1-33 Saunders Street and 20-28 Bulwara Road are flagged for larger mixed-use towers combining residential, commercial and retail. Developers benefiting from upzoning will contribute a 3 per cent levy on projects exceeding $1 million and a 9 per cent affordable housing contribution on new residential GFA created via rezoning, with the city projecting roughly $148 million in infrastructure funding by 2041. Council endorsement triggers ministerial review with new planning controls expected to take effect in 2026.

**Why it matters:** Pyrmont-Ultimo shows how an Australian council is using metro-led upzoning plus a 9% affordable-housing contribution to capture land-value uplift — a template for the next round of Sydney TOD precincts and a useful comparator for Melbourne's Arden and Fishermans Bend.
