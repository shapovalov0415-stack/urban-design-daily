## Homeowner Associations Are Standing in the Way of Heat Pumps

**Source:** Sightline Institute  
**URL:** https://www.sightline.org/2026/06/18/homeowner-associations-are-standing-in-the-way-of-heat-pumps/  
**Topics:** Urban Design

Sightline Institute argues that homeowner associations (HOAs) — the private covenants governing roughly a quarter of housing across the Cascadian region — have quietly become one of the largest barriers to residential heat-pump uptake. Many HOA boards retain decades-old aesthetic and noise restrictions that block exterior condensers, ducted retrofits and rooftop ground-source loops, even where state policy actively subsidises electrification under regional decarbonisation targets. The piece details how owner-occupiers in subdivisions across Oregon, Washington and British Columbia have been denied permits for code-compliant installations or hit with ongoing fines after installing units that meet manufacturer specifications. Sightline traces the legal mechanism — restrictive covenants that pre-date heat-pump technology yet remain enforceable absent state intervention — and reviews recent legislative attempts in California, Colorado and New York to pre-empt HOA bans on solar, EV charging and heat pumps. The recommended fix is a Cascadian version: a narrow statutory carve-out that prevents HOAs from prohibiting heat-pump installation while preserving reasonable rules around placement, screening and noise mitigation. The argument frames the issue as a planning rather than environmental fight: HOAs are private land-use governments, and electrification mandates fail when those governments hold a veto over the building stock they oversee.

**Why it matters:** HOA covenants quietly veto rooftop and yard-mounted heat pumps in subdivisions that house tens of millions of Americans, and Sightline's Cascadia framing applies directly to Australian strata schemes facing the same electrification timeline. The piece is a clean argument for state-level pre-emption of private land-use restrictions when they block climate-essential building upgrades.

## Victoria's townhouse fast-track expands into Inner Melbourne activity centres

**Source:** The Urban Developer  
**URL:** https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/victoria-housing-capacity-targets-inner-city-activity-centres-frankston  
**Topics:** Australia, Melbourne, Victoria, Planning Reform, Housing

The Allan Labor Government has expanded its deemed-to-comply Townhouse and Low-Rise Code into a new generation of inner-city activity centres, naming Frankston, Box Hill, Sunshine and other established centres as priority precincts for two- and three-storey by-right housing. The move pairs the code — which removed planning permits for compliant duplexes, townhouses and small apartment buildings — with new state-imposed dwelling-capacity targets that obligate councils to demonstrate the planning capacity to deliver their share of the National Housing Accord 1.2-million-homes commitment over five years. Officials briefed industry that early data from the first 12 months of the code has shown a roughly 50 percent jump in permit applications statewide, with the strongest response in Melbourne's middle suburbs (Monash 515 homes, Merri-bek 452, Banyule 402, Darebin 345). Extending the framework into activity centres aims to capture the next wave around train and tram nodes already zoned for jobs and retail, where Victoria's Plan for Victoria framework has long earmarked underused commercial floors and surface car parks for residential overlay. The package also signals a shift in oversight: councils that fall behind their capacity target may face state intervention, including ministerial call-in of stalled applications.

**Why it matters:** Victoria is now coupling a by-right design code with hard council-level capacity targets and an intervention backstop — the first Australian jurisdiction to attempt all three at once. The pattern is the closest thing to a national template for converting 'gentle density' policy into measurable dwelling counts.

## Asheville reconsiders parking minimums as surface parking eats over a third of downtown

**Source:** Planetizen  
**URL:** https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/06/137810-asheville-reconsiders-parking-minimums-surface-parking-eats-over-third-downtown  
**Topics:** United States, Parking Reform, Zoning, Urban Form, Housing

Planetizen reports that Asheville, North Carolina, is moving to overhaul its parking-minimum requirements after a city-commissioned audit found that surface parking now covers more than a third of downtown real estate — land that could otherwise host housing, retail, public realm or open space. The current code requires fixed minimum parking ratios for almost every land use, a holdover from suburban-style 1970s zoning that has compounded across decades to lock vast central-city parcels into low-value asphalt while the city faces an acute housing shortage. The proposed reform would eliminate minimum parking requirements citywide for commercial and mixed-use development, with the housing-focused districts going to zero immediately; large-format retail would phase out over three years. Planning staff modelled the change against five comparable Sunbelt cities that abolished minimums between 2017 and 2023, finding none experienced the predicted parking 'shortage' and most saw new housing supply within blocks of transit corridors. Local debate has been split between downtown property owners worried about overflow into residential streets and housing advocates pointing to the foregone rents and tax base sitting under uncovered car parks. Council is expected to vote on the package later this northern summer.

**Why it matters:** Asheville is mid-size enough that other Sunbelt and Australian regional councils will read the outcome as a proof-point: when a third of your downtown is asphalt, removing the rule that requires it is the cheapest housing intervention you can make.
